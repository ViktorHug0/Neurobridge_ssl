## Nash: the result and the mechanism

Nash-inspired training uses the bisector of normalized shared-backbone task
gradients, rescaled to the ordinary mean gradient norm, then passed to AdamW.
Private head gradients retain ordinary mean-loss scaling. This is not the full
paper's optimizer or step-size prescription.

- Fused top1:35.30 versus35.25 baseline, +.05pp;6 subject wins and4 losses.
- Individual heads:32.70/32.80 versus32.60/32.20 baseline.
- Oracle top1:42.40 versus41.65; distractor bonus.04522 versus.04453.
- Mean epoch time including validation:35.06 versus25.38 seconds.
- Recorded train+val time:5.86 versus4.23 hours, approximately39% more.

Gradient logs are more informative than the tiny accuracy difference:

| Training epochs | Mean shared-gradient cosine | Mean TS/ATM gradient-norm ratio |
|---|---:|---:|
|1--5|.799|1.012|
|6--20|.895|1.007|
|21--40|.901|.998|
|41 onward|.892|.986|

Only31 of78,731 training steps had negative overall gradient cosine (.0394%).
Neither the ordinary mean direction evaluated on these gradients nor the sampled
actual AdamW displacement had a negative task-improvement dot product in the
recorded diagnostics. Actual update diagnostics were sampled every ten steps.

Interpretation: there was little persistent whole-backbone conflict or average
norm imbalance for this rule to correct. This weakens gradient conflict as the
dominant explanation for the compact model's deficit. It does not rule out local
conflict, nor prove the unmodified baseline follows the same gradient trajectory:
these measurements are from Nash-trained models.

## New post-hoc comparison: ATM's value is complementary prediction

For this recap I combined existing score matrices using the already fixed50/50
row-z rule, without training, fitting weights or selecting epochs. Query/candidate
identity alignment was checked. The two pairs using the newly completed IV28 solo
are new descriptive comparisons, not preregistered confirmations.

| Pair | Mean individual top1 | Fused top1 | Either-member oracle | Top1 prediction agreement | Distractor bonus |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
|TSConv-IV33 + ATM-IV28|32.45|40.20|47.10|27.75|.13788|
|TSConv-IV33 + TSConv-IV28|32.50|36.70|42.95|43.60|.06904|

Replacing ATM-IV28 (33.40 solo) with TSConv-IV28 (33.50 solo) preserves average
individual accuracy but loses3.50pp of fused accuracy. The new pair agrees more,
has a lower either-member oracle, and has about half the distractor bonus.

Useful descriptive chain: heterogeneous independent40.20 -> same-family
independent36.70 -> shared same-family35.25. It suggests replacing the ATM
computation is a substantial part of the apparent compression gap. This is not
an exact causal decomposition: widths, initializations, selected epochs and
optimization histories are not all identical.

Even keeping both targets atIV28, the heterogeneous pair reaches38.60. Architecture
differences therefore provide useful complementarity beyond changing image layers.
The38.60 versus40.20 comparison also changes the TS width/target/training, so it
does not isolate a pure1.60pp effect of image-layer diversity.

## Electrode attention and distillation

Electrode attention raises both individual heads to33.40, but fused accuracy
reaches only35.50. Its bonus remains.04397 versus baseline.04453: stronger solos
do not translate into a stronger joint prediction. This particular branch design
did not reproduce the independent ATM's contribution.

Distillation did transfer teacher behavior. Recomputed on the held-out200-candidate
galleries, mean teacher-to-student KL falls from.05609 for baseline to.04809 for
distilled, and teacher top1 agreement rises52.60% ->54.15%.

The teacher is correct on804/2000 queries. Baseline versus distilled correct counts:

| Cases | Baseline | Distilled |
|---|---:|---:|
|Teacher correct|546|561|
|Teacher wrong|159|145|
|Total|705|706|

The net increase on teacher-correct cases is15, offset by14 fewer correct answers
on teacher-wrong cases. These are category totals, not claims that exactly those
individual query transitions occurred. The fixed distillation objective learned
similarity to the teacher without recovering its ensemble accuracy. No further
distillation is recommended here, consistent with the user's preference.

### Distractor intervention and repetition study

The wrong-score alignment intervention preserves each member's correct score and
its multiset of wrong scores, but makes their hardest distractors coincide:

| Committee | Original fused top1 | Aligned distractors |
|---|---:|---:|
|Three TSConv seeds|37.10|34.05|
|Diverse triple|43.80|30.30|

These label-informed synthetic interventions are mechanistic evidence, not usable
inference methods. In particular, their shuffled-distractor78--93% numbers must
not be reported as real model retrieval results. They demonstrate how much the
identity of wrong candidates can matter even with unchanged individual accuracy.

Repetition averaging remains essential. At1/8/32/80 repetitions, the diverse
triple reaches6.12/24.29/37.70/43.80, versus4.815/20.19/32.54/37.10 for three
TSConv seeds. Diversity helps across noise levels but does not replace EEG
repetition averaging. These historical committees use test-selected models and
different members; the comparison is not an isolated architecture intervention.

## Updated interpretation and priorities

1. Independent ATM predictions add useful complementary rankings even when a
   TSConv replacement has similar solo accuracy. This is now the strongest direct
   evidence explaining the heterogenous ensemble's value.
2. The studied shared model's task gradients are largely aligned and similarly
   sized. Nash balancing did not solve the problem and costs more.
3. IV28 solo training recovers a modest1.30pp over its ordinary shared head, with
   selection/scale caveats. This leaves most of the heterogeneous pair's benefit
   unexplained by a simple joint-training penalty.
4. Compatible output geometry exists: aligned128D fusion retains40%. The unresolved
   question is which private EEG computations are needed to generate that vector.
5. The prior same-family sharing experiment suggests much computation may be
   shareable. The ATM/TSConv-specific ladder remains the correct missing experiment,
   but it has not run successfully yet.

The useful next step is to repair and verify that ladder's implementation, then
run the native/compatible baseline gate before interpreting any sharing results.
Further blanket widening, attention additions or gradient balancing are lower
priority until that comparison locates the important private computation.
